On June 11, 2026, we held the first Regional Partnership Meeting of FY2026 at the Kina Community Center.

Participants joined us from many different fields, including education, local government, the council of social welfare, and the chamber of commerce. We sincerely thank everyone who took time from their busy schedules to support the children who will shape our future.

## Purpose of the Meeting and Review of the Previous Year

The meeting was held to evaluate the previous year’s results and share the operating policy for the new year. Our goal was to build a more organized and secure network for supporting children.

![Presentation slide explaining the purpose of the meeting](https://news.ryukyu-tane.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/スクリーンショット-2026-06-12-7.18.46.png)

The activity report described significant changes among students who took part in workplace experiences, including **returning to school and becoming able to greet others**.

At the same time, the review revealed two major challenges: a structure that had relied on **the goodwill of particular individuals**, and the need for stronger **legal and organizational risk management**.

## This Year’s Policy: From Goodwill to an Organized System

In response to the previous year’s challenges, we are reorganizing the project around three pillars.

1. **Support children’s independence and participation in society through the strength of the whole community**  
   Depending only on particular adults, such as parents or teachers, can create a risk of overdependence. The “Yomitan Network” will give children opportunities to meet a wider range of adults and distribute that risk.

   ![Presentation slide on community-wide support](https://news.ryukyu-tane.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/スクリーンショット-2026-06-12-7.19.20.png)

2. **Make changes in children’s actions visible and strengthen information sharing**  
   A point system will make a child’s growing sense of competence visible through actions such as attending school, joining a HOP class at the community center, or taking part in a local field visit. We also plan to improve information sharing by making use of school-issued tablets, building on the social-media tools used during the previous year.

   ![Presentation slide on visualizing progress and sharing information](https://news.ryukyu-tane.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/スクリーンショット-2026-06-12-7.19.22.png)

3. **Protect children through legal compliance and governance**  
   We will introduce a four-party consent agreement involving KCS as the organizer, parents or guardians, schools, and host organizations. This will create a system designed to safeguard children’s development. The program will operate on the premise that it complies with Japan’s Labor Standards Act and Child Welfare Act, while also pursuing seamless education and employment support for young people who do not enter high school or who leave school before graduating.

   ![Presentation slide on legal compliance and governance](https://news.ryukyu-tane.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/スクリーンショット-2026-06-12-7.19.24.png)

## The Yomitan Network: Supporting Children as a Whole Community

During the discussion, participants held an in-depth exchange about local challenges and the circumstances surrounding children today.

Participants strongly agreed that responsibility should not fall on one company or individual; the whole Yomitan community needs to receive and support children’s motivation. They also confirmed the need for a network of safe places for children and risk-management education suited to the present day.

## Looking Ahead

We will continue with the words of the Kina Community Association chair in mind: “When the community faces a problem, the whole community should solve it. We want the Yomitan Network to support our children.”

We will respect each child’s own decision—“I want to go”—and build a one-stop support structure connecting schools, families, and the community. We appreciate your continued understanding and support.
