On May 23, 2026, Unna Junior High School invited Kaname Sadoyama to give a PTA lecture. The theme was “Creating Opportunities That Unlock Children’s Potential: A Sense of Competence and Multiple Intelligences.” Many parents and guardians attended, making it a valuable opportunity to think together about children’s growth and future.

## A Sense of Competence and Multiple Intelligences

Representing Ryukyu no Tane, Sadoyama spoke about two ideas: a sense of competence—the feeling that “I can do this”—as a key that helps children face difficulty, and Howard Gardner’s theory of multiple intelligences as a way to recognize the different talents of every child.

Participants shared many thoughtful observations and positive intentions for their everyday relationships with their children.

## What Parents and Guardians Learned—and Want to Put into Practice

Seventeen people responded to the questionnaire. Three points appeared to resonate especially strongly.

- **Make listening a habit**  
  Many participants reaffirmed the importance of first listening carefully and accepting what a child says, rather than beginning with criticism.
- **Praise the process, not only the result**  
  Participants connected with the importance of focusing on a child’s effort and the process that led to a result, even when the outcome was unsuccessful.
- **Understand multiple intelligences**  
  Looking through the lens of eight different intelligences helped parents and guardians rediscover their children’s strengths, including spatial, interpersonal, and musical intelligence.

## Renewed Energy for PTA Activities

The lecture also inspired participants to suggest activities that could bring children’s ideas to life and revive some traditional PTA events.

- **Food:** a katsu-curry event before entrance examinations and more hands-on cooking activities
- **Sports and connection:** reviving sports days and volleyball tournaments, and creating more interaction with the community
- **Making things:** activities such as making muumuu dresses while experiencing Okinawan culture

## Overall Reflections

Participants offered warm comments such as, “This talk was worth paying for,” and, “My worries about how to relate to my child have cleared.” We are glad that the lecture gave parents and guardians renewed confidence in facing their children with care.

## Closing

We sincerely thank the parents and guardians who attended, as well as the PTA members who planned and organized the event.

We hope the lecture will become an opportunity for the students of Unna Junior High School to shine as themselves and develop a stronger sense of competence.

Ryukyu no Tane will continue supporting environments in which schools, families, and communities work together to open possibilities for children.
